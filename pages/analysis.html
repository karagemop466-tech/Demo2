Method

Trade analysis and strategy framework

The categorisation rules below are fixed before any outcome is known, and every input comes from an official Kalshi field. No result is reported until the underlying market has actually resolved, and no strategy is described as working until the measured numbers say so.

Nothing here is a validated trading strategy. These are labelling rules and a measurement framework for a demo portfolio. They have not been tested out-of-sample, they ignore the fee schedule unless a fee is supplied, and they are not investment advice. Kalshi demo outcomes are generated from mock data.

2 · How a trade is categorised

Each fill is given one label per dimension. The dimensions are chosen from official fields only, and they are applied at entry time — before the market resolves — so they cannot be tuned to the answer.

DimensionValuesOfficial field used
Topicgas-prices · elections · culture · crypto · golfSeries category / tags, else the category page
Sideyes · noFill side
Price bandlongshot (<25¢) · mid (25–60¢) · favourite (>60¢)Fill price in dollars
Horizonshort-dated (<2 days) · medium (2–30 days) · long (>30 days)Market close_time minus fill time
Entry side of the bookat-bid · at-mid · at-offerFill price against the orderbook at entry

The combination of the five values is the strategy label shown on the Portfolio & Trading page, for example crypto · YES · favourite (>60¢) · short-dated (<2d) · at-offer.

4 · What the public data already supports

These statements are supported by data on this site today and need no account:

  • Volume is denominated in contracts. The API returns volume_fp and volume_24h_fp as contract counts; there is no dollar-turnover field, so every dollar figure on this site is a labelled estimate (volume_fp × last_price_dollars). Flag F-10.
  • Outcome is binary and settled from the official result field. A settled market reports result = yes or no plus settlement_value_dollars; this is what the resolution table shows. Source.
  • Liquidity is observable but one-sided. The API publishes bids only; the implied offer is $1.00 − best NO bid, and an empty book is a normal response for an inactive market. Source.
  • Price history exists officially. Candlesticks are available per market at 1-minute, 1-hour or 1-day resolution, up to 5,000 periods per request. Source.
  • Demo data is synthetic. Markets such as “1+1 = 3” and “demo strike” contracts exist, and demo settlement outcomes are not evidence about real events. Source.

5 · Comparison discipline

When results do arrive, each category is compared against the portfolio as a whole and against a do-nothing baseline (the average entry price of every trade in the portfolio), so a category is only credited with the spread between its own result and that baseline. Results are read in settlement order, not in the order that looks best, and trades are not removed from a losing category after the fact.
