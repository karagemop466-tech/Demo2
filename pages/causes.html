Cause channels — where a sharp price move can be checked

The dashboard marks every move of $0.10 or more between two consecutive contract prices. Kalshi's API returns the price, the volume and the time of that move — it does not return the news behind it, and this site will not invent one.

What is and is not claimed here. Nothing on this page says an article caused a move. Each row is a channel to check, published by Kalshi itself as the settlement source for that contract, together with the market's own page so the move can be lined up against its timestamp. Verifying a cause is left to the reader, because the only authoritative statement of a cause would have to come from the source that settles the contract.

1. The official settlement sources, per contract

Every Kalshi series record carries a settlement_sources array (Get Series List). Those are the publications the exchange itself names as authoritative for the contract, which makes them the right places to look first. They come from the API — not from a keyword search.

2. How to line a move up against the news

StepWhat to doWhy that is the correct orderSource
1Read the move's timestamp on the dashboard (UTC to the second). The chart's points are timestamped by the exchange; a cause must be at or before the move. Candlesticks (end_period_ts)
2Open the market's own page and check its rules text. The rules define exactly which event would move the contract, so you know what to look for. Market lifecycle
3Look at the settlement sources listed for that series. Kalshi names these as authoritative for the contract; other outlets are commentary. Get Series List
4Check whether the move coincides with a trade print at that price. A jump with volume is a traded repricing; a jump with no volume is a quote change. Get Trades
5Record the link, the timestamp and the source. If nothing matches, leave it blank. An unmatched move stays "cause unverified" — the site never guesses. Flagged for Review

3. Suggested reading — channels, not conclusions

These are general newsrooms that Kalshi's own settlement_sources lists for in-scope series. They are suggestions of where to look, not evidence about any specific move, and no publication is claimed to have reported any specific jump.

4. What would make this automatic

Two things are missing before a jump could be annotated automatically, and both are recorded rather than worked around: Kalshi publishes no news or event feed keyed to price moves, and its public API returns no field describing why a price changed. Until one exists, this page stays a checklist. See Suggestions and Flagged for Review (F13).
