Trade analysis and strategy

Every trade is bucketed four ways — topic, side, entry-price band, and topic × side — and a bucket is only allowed to call itself a pattern once it holds at least 10 trades. Below that threshold the page says “insufficient data” instead of writing a strategy. Source of trades: checking… (0 rows).

A candidate is a description of what the data already did, not a forecast. Ten trades is a low bar chosen so the page is usable; it is not a statistical test, and nothing here has been fee-adjusted on the paper side.

What the numbers cannot tell you

  • Demo ≠ real. Kalshi states demo prices and behaviour may not reflect real markets, so an edge here may not exist on the live exchange (demo env).
  • Selection bias. Trades you chose to place are not a random sample of markets.
  • Survivorship. Fill data only covers markets that traded; quiet markets never appear.
  • No counterfactual. We never see what the alternative trade would have returned.
  • Small samples. Ten trades cannot separate skill from luck; treat any candidate as a hypothesis.

How to make a strategy decision anyway

  1. Log at least 10 trades per bucket you want to judge, or connect a demo account with real fills.
  2. Check the price band: an edge concentrated in 80¢+ contracts needs very few losses to vanish.
  3. Subtract fees before believing a positive bucket.
  4. Compare against doing nothing — the honest baseline for a demo is 0% return.
  5. Only then choose a path on ; nothing here is auto-applied.

Open questions that block a firmer answer are listed with discovery steps on , chiefly Q-3 (minimum sample) and Q-5 (fee schedule).
