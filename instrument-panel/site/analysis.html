Analysis

What the collected data can actually support — including one correction that changes the answer. The first version of this page calibrated every resolved market. That was wrong, and the reason is on this page: most resolved demo markets never traded, so the price attached to them is a default, not a quote.

Demo environment data — not real markets

Every price, volume and resolution below comes from Kalshi's demo exchange (external-api.demo.kalshi.co). Kalshi states that demo prices and behaviour “may not be reflective of those in real markets”. See demo environment docs. No real money is involved anywhere on this site.

0. The correction: a resolved market is not necessarily a priced market

1,472 of 1,601 resolved in-scope markets never traded

A settled market with volume_fp = 0 comes back with last_price_dollars = 0.0000 (values observed in this snapshot: 0.0000). Treating that zero as “the market priced this at 0%” and then observing that 219 of them (14.9%) resolved YES produces a fake edge of that full amount against a supposed 0% price. It measures Kalshi's default, not the market's opinion. The calibration below therefore uses only markets that actually traded (129 of 1,601). The never-traded population is reported next to it, for transparency, and is excluded from the bands.

1. Calibration of traded, resolved markets

Method (reproducible from the published data)

For each resolved market with volume_fp > 0, take its last traded YES price, put it in a 10-cent band, and compare the band's mean price with the share of those markets that resolved YES. Prices are already the market's own implied probability, so a calibrated band has mean ≈ realised rate. Arithmetic only. A band with n < 30 is labelled too small to interpret, because it is.

4. What cannot be concluded, and why

“This topic is more profitable.”Profitability needs a portfolio. There are no executed trades, so no realised P&L exists. Calibration of market prices is not the same thing as trade performance.
“Long shots are underpriced.”The apparent edge in the low bands comes mostly from never-traded markets, and the remaining traded low-band sample is still small relative to its spread. See section 0.
“Demo behaviour predicts the real market.”Kalshi states demo prices and behaviour may not be reflective of real markets (demo_env).
“Volume shows dollar turnover.”Volume is contracts. Dollar figures here are contracts × price and are labelled as derived (Get Markets).
“A wide gap is free money.”Fees, spread and depth all reduce it; the orderbook is bids-only, so the true ask is 1 − best opposite bid, and many markets here have an empty book. See Live & liquidity.
“The resolved set is complete.”Resolved rows are capped per topic and only series inside the scope queries are read. Counts are a lower bound. See Verification.
