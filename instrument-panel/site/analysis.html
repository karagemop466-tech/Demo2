Analysis

What the collected data can actually support. Two things are true at once: the market data is rich enough to measure whether prices were calibrated, and it is not rich enough to recommend a trade. This page keeps that line visible.

Demo environment data — not real markets

Every price, volume and resolution below comes from Kalshi's demo exchange (external-api.demo.kalshi.co). Kalshi states that demo prices and behaviour “may not be reflective of those in real markets”. See demo environment docs. No real money is involved anywhere on this site.

1. Price calibration of resolved in-scope markets

Method (reproducible from the published data)

Take every resolved in-scope market, bucket it by its last traded YES price, and compare the band's mean price with the share of those markets that actually resolved YES. A market priced at $0.70 should resolve YES about 70% of the time; if the realised rate is far below that, the band was expensive. Arithmetic only — no model is fitted, nothing is predicted.

5. What cannot be concluded, and why

“This topic is more profitable.”Profitability needs a portfolio. There are no executed trades, so no realised P&L exists. Market-level calibration is not the same thing as trade performance.
“Demo behaviour predicts the real market.”Kalshi states demo prices and behaviour may not be reflective of real markets (demo_env).
“Volume shows dollar turnover.”Volume is contracts. Dollar figures here are contracts × price and are labelled as derived (Get Markets).
“A wide price gap is free money.”Fees, spread and depth all reduce it; the orderbook is bids-only, so the true ask is 1 − best opposite bid. See the Live page.
